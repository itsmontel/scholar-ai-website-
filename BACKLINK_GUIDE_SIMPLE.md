# Backlinks Explained Like You're Five

## What Is a Backlink? (The Simple Version)

Imagine you have a lemonade stand. When another kid says "Hey, that stand over there has great lemonade!" and points to you, more people come to your stand.

A **backlink** is when another website "points" to your website. It's like a digital recommendation. When a big, trusted website links to WriteScholar, Google thinks: "If they think WriteScholar is good, maybe it is." So Google shows your site higher when people search for things like "essay checker" or "study tools."

**Why it matters:** Right now you only have links from your own sites (Mosaic, VirtualPaint, etc.). That's like your mom saying your lemonade is great. Google wants to hear from *other* people too. When education sites, AI directories, or student blogs link to you, Google trusts you more.

# How to Get Backlinks: Two Ways

## Way 1: Fill Out Forms (Easy — Do These First)

Some websites have a simple form. You fill it out, they add your link. No email needed.

## Way 2: Send Emails (Outreach)

Some websites have lists like "Best Study Tools" or "Best Essay Checkers." You email them and say: "Hey, can you add WriteScholar to your list?" They say yes, you get a backlink.

# HARO (Help A Reporter Out) — Get Free Press Backlinks

**What it is:** Journalists post questions like "I need an expert on study tips for students." You answer. If they use your quote, you get a mention + backlink in their article (e.g. Forbes, Time).

**How to sign up:** Go to [helpareporter.com](https://www.helpareporter.com/) and sign up as a **source** (not a journalist). You'll get 3 emails per day with journalist queries.

**Tip:** Filter for "education," "students," "AI tools," "study tips." Reply fast (within 1–2 hours). Only reply if you're a genuine fit.

**Basic plan:** Free, but limited. Paid ($19/mo) gives keyword alerts and better placement.
