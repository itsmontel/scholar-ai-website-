Your first college paper is not a warm-up.

Classes just started back. That week-three assignment is how the course decides what kind of writer you are. Here is a 3-week plan so paper one is not the grade you spend October recovering from.

Read the 3-week plan →

The plan, week by week

Week 1

Claim + sources

Answer the prompt in one sentence a classmate could disagree with. Name the sources you are allowed to use.

Week 2

Ugly draft

Write the body first. Each paragraph: a claim, evidence, and a sentence that ties it back.

Week 3

Grade it first

Read the rubric again. Fix the two weakest categories. Citations next. Grammar last.

What first-years get wrong in September

They write the paper they wanted — not the one that was assigned. Underline the verb in the prompt. That verb is the assignment.

They pad. High school rewarded length. College readers reward a claim that arrives on time.

They cite like it is optional. Missing page numbers get noticed on paper one, because the professor is still marking them.

They submit the first readable draft. Readable is not the same as argued. If a roommate can name the topic but not the claim, you are not done.

Night-before checklist

1.  Your thesis answers the prompt, not a neighbouring question.

2.  Every body paragraph has a claim and a source.

3.  In-text citations match the reference list and style.

4.  You are inside the word count — not fashionably over it.

5.  You uploaded the version you just read, not FinalFinal2.
